# Reportable incidents — advisory tables (Australia)

Read only the section for the framework the user chose. Quote its caveat verbatim before
reporting against it. These tables help a user spot a *possible* match and the notification
window that would run if it is one. They do not decide reportability, and nothing in
ShiftCare notifies a regulator.

## NDIS disability supports

> This is a summary of the reportable-incident types the NDIS Quality and Safeguards
> Commission requires **registered** NDIS providers to notify. Whether a given event meets a
> type, and the deadline that applies, is decided by your organisation's incident lead against
> the Commission's current guidance at
> [ndiscommission.gov.au](https://www.ndiscommission.gov.au/rules-and-standards/reportable-incidents-and-incident-management/reportable-incidents),
> not by this record. Unregistered providers have different obligations. This is not legal
> advice.

Notification is to the Commission, by the provider, through the Commission's portal. The
ShiftCare **NDIS report status** field only records whether that has been done.

| Reportable incident type | Typical signs in a report | Window after becoming aware |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Death of a person with disability | Any death during or connected with supports, expected or not | 24 hours, then the 5-business-day form |
| Serious injury | Fracture, head injury, burns, injury needing hospital or medical treatment, injury from a fall | 24 hours, then the 5-business-day form |
| Abuse or neglect | Rough handling, withheld care, missed medication with harm, financial abuse, humiliation, failure to act on a known risk | 24 hours, then the 5-business-day form |
| Unlawful sexual or physical contact, or assault | Any allegation of assault or unlawful contact, by anyone, in connection with supports | 24 hours, then the 5-business-day form |
| Sexual misconduct, including grooming | Sexual conduct committed against, or in the presence of, the person | 24 hours, then the 5-business-day form |
| Use of a restrictive practice not in accordance with a state or territory authorisation, or not in a behaviour support plan | Chemical, mechanical, physical or environmental restraint, seclusion, without authorisation or outside the plan | 5 business days — **24 hours if it caused harm** |

Not every incident is reportable. A near miss, a fall without injury, or a medication error
without harm still belongs in the incident register and in the provider's incident
management system; it may not need Commission notification. Say that the facts as described
do not match a type; do not say it is not reportable.

## Aged care and home care — Serious Incident Response Scheme (SIRS)

> This is a summary of the reportable-incident types under the Serious Incident Response
> Scheme, which covers residential aged care and, since 1 December 2022, home and community
> aged care. The Aged Care Act 2024 and Aged Care Rules 2025 changed the scheme from 1 November
> 2025, so confirm the current types, priorities and windows with the Aged Care Quality and
> Safety Commission at
> [agedcarequality.gov.au](https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/providers/serious-incident-response-scheme)
> before relying on this. Your incident lead decides. This is not legal advice.

Notification is to the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission, by the provider, through the
Commission's portal. ShiftCare's **NDIS report status** field is not a SIRS field; leave it
at its default and say it does not apply.

| Reportable incident type | Typical signs in a report |
| --- | --- |
| Unreasonable use of force | Hitting, pushing, shoving, rough handling |
| Unlawful sexual contact or inappropriate sexual conduct | Any sexual contact or conduct without consent, or by staff |
| Psychological or emotional abuse | Yelling, name-calling, humiliation, threats, ignoring |
| Unexpected death | Death not reasonably expected, including from a fall or an error in care |
| Stealing or financial coercion by a staff member | Missing money or property, pressure to give money or gifts |
| Neglect | Failure to provide care that leaves the person at risk or harmed, including missed medication or missed visits with consequence |
| Inappropriate use of a restrictive practice | Restraint or seclusion not in line with the person's plan or consent |
| Unexplained absence from care | The person missing from where they should be, and the provider cannot account for it |

| Priority | Meaning | Window after becoming aware |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Priority 1 | Caused, or could reasonably have caused, physical or psychological injury or discomfort needing medical or psychological treatment; or there are reasonable grounds to report to police; **and every** unlawful sexual contact or inappropriate sexual conduct, unexpected death, or unexplained absence | **24 hours** |
| Priority 2 | A reportable incident that does not meet Priority 1 | 30 days |

Falls are the commonest home-care incident. A fall is not itself one of the eight types; it
becomes reportable when it reveals one — neglect, unexpected death, injury from an unreasonable
use of force — so record it fully and let the incident lead judge.

## Other, or the organisation's own policy

No regulator table applies from this file. Draft the record in the same shape, ask who the
organisation's incident lead is and whether their policy sets a notification window, and put
that in **Actions taken** as outstanding follow-up. Providers outside Australia have their own
regulators and schemes; the user's policy names them, this file does not.
